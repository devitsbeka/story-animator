# Cinematic quality operating standard

Film quality is evaluated across 12 dimensions: story/attention, shot grammar, camera motion, believable set density, models/UV, physically plausible shading, motivated lighting, temporal animation, atmosphere, compositing/color, sound design, and editorial pacing. For realistic people add skin/eyes/teeth, hair, expression, lipsync, body contact and performance continuity.

## Review stages

1. Graybox blocking contact sheet: composition, camera, scale, timing.
2. Material/light look-dev: physically plausible contact sheet and lighting consistency.
3. Low-cost motion preview: evaluate animation interpolation, camera smoothness and intersections.
4. Final frames: highest-risk frames (close-ups, high reflection, atmosphere, human faces).
5. Encoded video: motion, story pace, captions, audio synchronization.

## Rejection examples

Camera clips through building; flat color facades marketed as photoreal; UV stretching; visible primitive geometry on hero objects; shadows detached; random zooms unrelated to narration; fake lens flare that obscures action; misaligned lip movements; repeating background props; inconsistent era and asset scale; mechanical linear movement; dead-silent soundscape; cinematic frames but unusable captions.

## Gate categories

- **Hard fail:** missing asset, corrupt render, out-of-frame key subject, black frames, missing audio, clipping, noncompliant entitlement.
- **Needs repair:** unmotivated lighting, sterile materials, jitter, bad typography, visual era mismatch, weak transitions.
- **Human review:** emotionally convincing performance, genuinely premium look, storytelling, historical accuracy, final brand standard.

No numerical score alone proves Hollywood quality. Require rendered video exemplars and an accountable reviewer decision for premium production.
