# Craft Canon: source list (from the user, 2026-11-22)
These sources are the foundation of how this engine makes films. Each one is distilled into rules (factory/canon/*.md) that are enforced in the engines and gates.

1. Storytelling Through Lighting, Sharon Calahan (Pixar): https://www.terryflaxton.com/uploads/2/8/9/0/28906783/storytelling_through_lighting_2.pdf
   Light directs the eye, creates depth, serves the shot's emotional purpose. At night, decide what the viewer discovers and when.
2. Lighting, Cinematography Mailing List (printed p.138 "Filming at night"): https://www.cinematography.net/Files/CML-Lighting.pdf
   Large night exteriors, practicals in frame, wide-to-close continuity (the close-up must not suddenly feel like daylight).
3. Basic Cinematography for Animation, UW CSE456 (two-shot exercise p.1): https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse456/18su/resources/Basic%20Cinematography%20for%20Animation.pdf
   The wide establishes geography; the close-up is saved for the reaction the scene builds toward.
4. The Technique of Film Editing, Reisz & Millar (Ch.3 "Action Sequences"): https://eclass.asfa.gr/modules/document/file.php/DIGITALARTS121/%5BKarel_Reisz%2C_GAVIN_MILLAR%5D_Technique_of_Film_Editting.pdf
   Order of information in action: obstacle seen (audience) -> seen (character) -> impact -> cost discovered.
5. Paul Greengrass, CCEA director study: https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/Support/Fact%20Files%3A%20Practitioners/2020/A2%20MIA%20Fact%20File%3A%20Paul%20Greengrass.pdf
   Handheld that reacts: the subject briefly escapes frame on impact; the camera catches up a fraction late.
6. Animation Tips & Tricks Vol.1, Animation Mentor ("Expression Changes Get Lost in Movement"): https://content.animationmentor.com/pdfs/TipsAndTricks_Volume1.pdf
   Don't hide the key expression change inside a head turn: turn, settle, THEN the thought lands on the face.
7. Russell Carpenter ASC, American Cinematographer: https://theasc.com/article/russell-carpenter-asc-passion-for-the-craft/
   Preview practicals on water early (ship model + Mylar): test boat + one deck lamp + water before spray/weather/animation.
8. Remaking The 39 Steps, Hitchcock's screenwriting (printed p.40 "bomb underneath the table"): https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/127923/2/02whole.pdf
   Suspense = the audience knows the danger and time is running out; show the threat first.
9. Hitchcock, dramatic irony, shared suspense, CCEA MIA marking guide ("Shared Suspense"/"Rear Window"): https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/Past-Papers/cleared/GCE/GCE%20Moving%20Image%20Arts%20%282016%29/2019-Summer/Standard/0/GCE-Moving%20Image%20Arts-564-Summer2019-AS%202%2C%20Critical%20Response-MS.pdf
   Who knows and who can act changes the audience's question ("will he look up?" -> "can he turn in time?").
10. Directing the Documentary, Rabiger, Ch.33 "Narration" (pp.445-446 + "The Scripted Narration"): https://bpb-ap-se2.wpmucdn.com/mediafactory.org.au/dist/1/930/files/2017/07/Michael_Rabiger_Directing_The_Documentar-28p859l.pdf
    Who speaks, their authority, testing the read against picture; key word before/after the reveal; leave room for breath.
11. How to Write a Documentary Script, Trisha Das (p.42 narration, p.51 worked script): https://www.gires.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/programme_doc_documentary_script_Unesco.pdf
    Three parallel columns (PICTURE | NARRATION | SOUND); sound beats before lines; gaps for actor/sound.
