# Ten cinematic editing principles — complete mandatory design catalog

Apply as a compositional language across the entire piece. For each candidate scene, evaluate semantic suitability, subject legibility, text hierarchy, motion purpose, texture continuity, contrast, and retention. All scenes follow the visual system, but NOT all ten scene types must appear in a particular video.

## P01 — Captions and typography
**Meaning:** transform short spoken fragments into purposeful typographic hierarchies, not universal center subtitles. Inter (or metric-compatible clean sans) for ordinary words; Playfair Display Italic (or analogous licensed serif) for emphasized words. Usually <=5 words visible in each designed phrase. Choose one or two anchor words; larger/weightier near visual center or focal point. Place contextual secondary words above/below and in lateral negative space at smaller scale; avoid regular repeated fixed templates. Speech-synced reveals with subtle easing, <=250ms when quick. Keep face/speaker clear and captions readable. Allow intentional occasional plain subtitles for fast speech or accessibility; create separate verbatim SRT.
**Avoid:** five equally sized words on one baseline; arbitrary emphasis; random font switching; tiny type; constant bouncing.

## P02 — Semantic B-roll + text
Find footage that literally, metaphorically, or emotionally shows the spoken phrase. Composition: subject primary, soft vignette at edges, restrained paper texture as overlay, optional subtle peripheral blur; contrast preserved around human face/object. Text occupies negative space, using P01, not obstructing main subject. Motion can have modest parallax, slow push or match-cut driven by meaning. Avoid unlicensed or irrelevant clip filler.

## P03 — Painting + text
Select an appropriate art image or create original painterly art. Use grain, dry pigment texture, restrained rough-paper surface; edge treatment unifies painting with background. Place text around focal figure/action. Introduce with 0.25–0.7s camera glide (adjust to pace) and gentle hold. Preserve original artwork's fidelity; never simulate an actual source if unavailable. Avoid gratuitous pan, muddy contrast and thick paper overlay.

## P04 — Line + text
Monochrome white, warm paper or charcoal backdrop with subtle fibrous paper noise. A single cobalt/azure accent line grows along a planned path. Typography interrupts/breaks the line physically rather than sitting accidentally near it. Camera or viewport movement follows the path so the viewer experiences logical sequence in narration order. Lines, words, and motion are tied to word timestamps. Clean anti-aliasing; no default overglow.

## P05 — Line + illustration
Build on P04. Path can be horizontal with small pale anchor dot and illustration below; OR zigzag traveling between alternating illustrations to show ordered stages. Use simple drawn artwork matching rough muted editorial style. Link direction to narrative sequence. Preserve readable text and clear anchor relationships. The camera follows progression, not arbitrary keyframes.

## P06 — Text-centric quote
Background white/warm paper or cobalt, lightly roughened; paired visibly oversized opening/closing quotation marks framing the central quote. Important phrase has visual priority, other words balanced. Reveal phrase-by-phrase with restrained tracking/opacity, optionally slight vertical staging. Use for thesis, payoff, reflection, not every sentence. Ensure quotes are truly attributed or quote marks serve clear stylistic function.

## P07 — Central illustration
A textured painted, carved or cloth-like visual anchors composition. Often bleeds from bottom through mid-frame, leaving upper/lateral text areas. Irregular outer edge fades/tears into matching background. Variants: expressive person/profile, brain/top-down conceptual form, circular thin-line pattern. Illustration must relate to narration; text hugs negative space without overlap. No generic emoji/clip-art substitution for premium visual.

## P08 — Side illustration
Visual element occupies one side; explanatory type on the other with carefully aligned vertical rhythm. Alternative: rough semicircle at left with small typography orbiting it. Useful for contrasting two related elements or when text and figure need simultaneous comprehension. Balance visual weight and intentional empty space; never simply shrink P07 and pin to an edge.

## P09 — Object + text callout
A single relevant object is focal: instrument, artifact, symbolic object or prop. Text positions are purposeful and restrained. Reveal object via mask, gentle scale and texture wipe; tie labels to details or narrative role. Use only when specific object advances story. Prefer photographed/illustrated credible object with rights.

## P10 — Graphs and statistics
Dark flat background with subtle grain and thin light grid. Plot truthful graph using actual quantitative evidence or explicitly label conceptual charts as schematic. Thin crisp plotted line, modest glow, electric-blue traveling tracker/dot keyed to speech. Camera follows tracker where appropriate; avoid disorienting zooms. Graph transitions align with verbal sequence (e.g. successive 'up' creates successive steps). Always check scale, axes, units and labels. No invented statistics.

## A-ROLL scene type — human anchor
Uninterrupted speaker on camera, with P01 captions as needed. Put it at introduction of a new category, emotionally resonant moments, transitions after complex sequences, trust-building, and conversational jokes. Don't replace all face time with B-roll.

## Story cadence
- First few seconds: face / hook; introduce first vivid visual shortly after the viewer understands the subject.
- Change visual mode only with a meaningful speech beat; don't force a cut every N seconds.
- After a high-complexity animation, offer a calmer face or simpler design scene.
- Illustrate examples; show speaker introducing categories; use visual graphs for comparisons/progression; quote typography for a final memorable claim.
- Music and transitions should reinforce rather than conceal the semantic hierarchy.
