# Stranded at North Sentinel: what do you actually do?

Format: 9:16 survival explainer, about 75 s. High-energy direct-address voiceover. One continuous camera (no hard cuts).
The series hero is a solo sailor: white round head, red sweater.

## Facts used (checked 2026-11-21)
- India bars approach within about 5 nautical miles of North Sentinel (A&N Islands Protection of Aboriginal Tribes
  Regulation, 1956). The Indian Coast Guard and Navy patrol the zone. Most sources say 5 nm, one says 3 nm, so the script says
  "patrols these waters" and doesn't state a number aloud.
- The zone is mainly a public-health barrier: the Sentinelese have little or no immunity to outside diseases.
- MV Primrose ran aground on the North Sentinel reef on 2 Aug 1981 in a storm. Armed islanders gathered on the shore.
  The crew stayed inside the steel hull and were airlifted out by helicopter (ONGC-contracted) after several days.
  Accounts give 3 days to a week and 20 to 31 crew, so the script says "for days" and "every single one".
- The Sentinelese are never shown. Their presence is implied only by firelight in the trees. No caricature.

## Narration (ElevenLabs eleven_v4; tags in brackets are performance directions, not spoken)

[excited] You're alone on a yacht... in the middle of the night... and your engine just DIED.

[panicked] No lights. No power. Nothing.

[dramatic] And the current is dragging you toward the ONE island on Earth you are not allowed to touch.

[excited] North Sentinel Island!

[serious] The people who live here have refused contact with the outside world for thousands of years. And they defend that. Hard.

[dramatic] Then... crunch. You hit the reef. You're stuck.

[excited] So what do you actually do? Step one!

[shouting] Mayday! Grab your radio, channel sixteen, and fire off your emergency beacon. The Indian Coast Guard patrols these waters.

[excited] Step two: do NOT go ashore. Not for a photo. Not to say hi.

[serious] They have almost no immunity to our germs. Even a common cold could be deadly for them.

[excited] Step three: stay with your boat. And this has actually happened before!

[storytelling] In 1981, a cargo ship called the Primrose ran aground right here. The crew saw armed islanders on the beach... so they stayed inside their steel hull, and waited for days... until helicopters lifted every single one of them out.

[whispers] Step four: kill the lights. Stay quiet. Leave nothing behind.

[excited] And if the tide lifts your boat? Drift out past the reef, and get gone!

[dramatic] Because on North Sentinel... the smartest way to survive... is to never be there at all.
